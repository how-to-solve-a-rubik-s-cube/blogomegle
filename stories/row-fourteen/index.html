A Hundred Meetings · Part 9

Row Fourteen

Nov 23, 2026 · 3 min read · Priya & Alan · Dallas

The gate agent said forty minutes, then ninety, then "we will know more soon," which Priya translated as: buy the expensive water. She was thirty-eight, in row fourteen on a ticket that had already been changed once. Alan was forty-two and in the seat beside hers, a fact neither of them had chosen and both of them were now going to inhabit.

"I am going to talk," he said, "because if I sit in silence for two hours I will invent a problem with the wing. I am Alan. Forty-two. Tell me if you would rather have the problem with the wing."

"Priya. Thirty-eight. Talk. The wing is a professional's business. I draw packaging for things that do not need to be pretty and then are."

The airline interrupted them with a chime and a new time. They started again. Alan restored furniture for hotels and had opinions about veneer that he kept short, sensing the boundary. Priya was flying home from a client who had used the word synergy and then asked her to make a box look like trust. She was tired in a specific way. He did not try to cure it with a pep talk. He offered her the charger that still had some cord left, and she took it.

They were interrupted by a delay, a cart, a crying child who was not theirs and not their job, and a woman who needed the aisle. Each time, one of them said, "You were saying," and the other actually was. Priya told him about a father in Houston who called on Sundays and spoke mostly about tomatoes. Alan told her he had been divorced for three years and had kept the dog and lost the house, and that the dog was the better negotiator. She laughed at the right size.

When the plane finally boarded, row fourteen was real. The conversation became smaller because of elbows. Somewhere over a darkness that might have been Oklahoma, Priya said she hated landing small talk that pretended the flight had been a portal. Alan said, "Then we will not pretend. I am in Dallas through Thursday. If you want to finish the tomato story over a bad airport coffee tomorrow, I will be at the shop in the main terminal at eight. If you do not, the charger was still a fair trade."

She came at eight. The coffee was bad. The tomato story ended with her father winning a ribbon he pretended not to care about. Alan showed her a photo of the dog, because he was not above an obvious move, and then put the phone away when she had seen it once. They spent the layover that was no longer a layover walking the terminal the way people walk a neighborhood. Priya missed nothing important. Her flight home was the next one, and she made it.

They are not a couple who say the delay was destiny. The airline was disorganized. They were two adults who refused to let a chime throw away a good sentence. Thursday became a habit of calls, then a weekend, then a plan to look at apartments with better light for drawing. The dog approved, or at least did not file a dissent. Row fourteen remains a seat number. The part they kept is the phrase you were saying, used in kitchens now, with no plane attached.

End.
