A Hundred Meetings · Part 12

Three Voicemails

Nov 24, 2026 · 3 min read · Saoirse & Cian · Dublin

The first voicemail was a dentist. Saoirse deleted it and made the appointment anyway, because she was twenty-eight and had decided to be a person who handled small dread on the day it arrived. The second was a survey. She deleted that one with pleasure.

The third was Cian. He was thirty-two, and his voice did the thing it had always done, which was to sound as if he had walked somewhere before calling, so the words would be in the right order. They had almost married at twenty-six and thirty. The almost had ended over a job in Cork that he had wanted and she had not wanted to follow, and over her refusal to pretend the city was a small detail. They had been right to stop. They had also been unfinished, which is a different condition.

"I am not calling to restart the wedding," he said on the recording. "I am calling because your mother's neighbor said you were back in Dublin, and I have a box of your records in my cupboard, and the cupboard is being painted. I can leave them at the shop on Capel Street on Thursday. If you would rather I post them, say so. I hope you are well. I mean that in the plain way."

She listened twice. Then she called, which she had not done in two years.

"Thursday," Saoirse said. "I will come. I am not bringing a speech. I want the records, and I want to see your face when you hand them over, so I know which version of us is doing the handing."

"The current version," Cian said. "I will be the man in the gray coat. I am taller than the problem and shorter than the doorway."

The shop was a record shop only in the afternoons. In the mornings it sold coffee and kept its opinions quiet. Cian had the box on a stool. Inside were albums she had forgotten and one she had missed with a specificity that annoyed her. They stood between the bins. He looked like himself, only less certain he was about to be punished, which she preferred.

"I took the Cork job," he said. "It was the right job and the wrong way to leave. I am back. I am not asking you to be impressed."

"I am not impressed," Saoirse said. "I am glad you did not pretend the box was an accident. Have you eaten?"

They ate soup around the corner, at a table that tipped unless someone kept a hand on it. Cian kept his hand on it. Saoirse told him about a research post at the library that was not glamorous and was hers. He told her the records had stayed in the cupboard because throwing them away would have been a lie about what he had liked. She accepted the distinction.

They did not get married that spring. They did something more accurate. They went to hear a band, then another, and Saoirse kept her own flat. Cian learned to ask which city a plan assumed. She learned he could paint a cupboard without turning it into a metaphor. The third voicemail stayed in her phone until the storage filled, and then she let it go, because the man was no longer a message. He was across the table, holding the wobble still.

End.
