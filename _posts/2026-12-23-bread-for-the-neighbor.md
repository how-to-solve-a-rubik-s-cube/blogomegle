The bakery had doubled the order and Julien had not checked the slip. He stood in his own kitchen with four loaves and a dignity problem. He was forty-two. Across the landing, Helene was home; he knew because of the radio and the hour. He knocked with a loaf in each hand like a man surrendering to a farce.

"I was optimistic and then I was not," he said. "I am Julien. Eat one, freeze one, refuse both. I will survive the refusal. I may not survive four breakfasts."

Helene had flour on her wrist already. She was forty-five and had been making a tart that did not need his bread and might forgive it. She looked at the loaves and then at his face, which was asking for nothing but a destination.

"Bring them in," she said. "I am Helene. We will slice one and you will tell me whether you always accept extra bread from strangers. If you do, I will be careful. If you do not, we can eat."

"I do not. This is a surplus, not a method."

They ate. The tart and the bread were a better meal than either had planned, which they acknowledged without turning the acknowledgment into a vow. Helene edited textbooks. Julien maintained the clocks in a station, a job that made him precise about the rest of his life and strangely helpless before bakeries. She liked the combination. He liked that she had asked a clear question before she let him sit.

The other loaves went to her freezer and to a neighbor downstairs who had a new baby and no time. Julien did the carrying. Helene did the note. It was a small, competent partnership and it stirred both of them more than the flour had.

"Come back for the last slice on Sunday," Helene said. "Not with bread. With yourself and a time you can keep. If the clocks and the textbooks cannot meet, we will know early."

They met. The clocks and the textbooks could. Sunday became a standing slice and then a standing key, given after a winter of knocking, because the landing was cold and the trust was not. Julien still does not take extra bread on purpose. When it happens, there is a door that opens, an apron, a question, and a man who answers it honestly. Helene keeps a loaf in the freezer for the weeks the order is correct and they want the ritual anyway. Some surpluses are accidents. The ones that matter are repeated on purpose, with butter, at a table built for two.
