The outage took the office at two-fourteen, which Dev knew because he wrote it down. He was thirty. The screens went black and the room made the sound of a hundred people discovering they had no analog plan. Rachel had a notebook in her bag because she did not trust a calendar she could not spill coffee on. She was thirty-three. She tore out a page and started a grid of who needed to tell a client what.

"You, call the east team on a real phone," she said to Dev, because he was nearest and because he had already stood up, which she took as a usable trait. "Write the result. Do not tell me you will remember it."

"I will write it," Dev said. "I am Dev. I was going to introduce myself after the emergency. The emergency is winning."

"Rachel. The emergency can have ten minutes. Then we are people again."

They worked the ten minutes and then another twenty, on paper, in a stairwell where the light was better. Dev's handwriting was terrible and sincere. Rachel's was architectural. Between calls they learned the outlines: he worked on the databases, she worked on the contracts, they had been in the same building for a year and had spoken about printers. When the power returned, a small cheer happened and the moment passed. Rachel looked at the page.

"I am keeping this," she said. "Not as a souvenir. As a list we should finish tomorrow, in the daylight, with the clients who still need the truth. If you want to finish it over something that is not vending-machine pretzels, I am free at six."

"Six," Dev said. "I will bring my own pen. Thirty. I am only good in a crisis if someone tells me where to stand. You told me. I liked knowing."

At six the city had power and they had noodles. The list got finished. A second list began, not of clients, of days they were both free in the next month, written in the notebook because Rachel wanted it on paper and Dev wanted to be in the notebook. It was a plain desire and he said it plainly. She turned the book so he could write his name on a Thursday.

The Thursday held. The outage became a story they told once and then stopped telling, because the notebook had newer pages. Dev still works with databases. Rachel still prefers a thing she can spill on. Their shared flat has a drawer full of chargers and one notebook that is not a backup. It is the original. Dates, groceries, a question, an answer. The power can go whenever it likes. They already know where the pen is.

**End.**
