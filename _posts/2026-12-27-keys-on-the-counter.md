The keys had been there for months: Leah's spare on Adam's hook, Adam's spare in Leah's drawer, exchanged one rainy week when both of them traveled and the plants were hostages. She was thirty-one. He was thirty-four. They had been friends since a job that had ended, and the friendship had been the better institution.

Other people had come and gone. Leah's last boyfriend had disliked the spare key and said so, and had been invited to dislike it from somewhere else. Adam's last girlfriend had called Leah a complication. Leah was not a complication. She was a person who watered plants and told the truth. Adam had ended that chapter with a clarity he was proud of and also tired from.

On a Sunday the keys were simply on the counter between them, two sets, and a silence arrived that was not awkward yet.

"We keep acting surprised," Leah said. "I am not surprised. I trust you with the door. I would like to stop pretending the trust is only horticultural. If you want the same thing, say it before I make more coffee. If you do not, we will label the keys and survive."

Adam turned his mug once. "I want the same thing," he said. "I did not want to be the friend who waits for your life to empty out and then arrives as a plan. Your life emptied because those people were wrong, not because I was hovering. I was trying very hard not to hover. I am done with the performance. Keep my key. I will keep yours. We can change the reason."

They did not kiss over the hardware as a scene. They went for a walk in the ordinary drizzle and came back and cooked, and the cooking was different by a degree only they would measure. The friends who had called it from the beginning were unbearable and correct. Leah told them so with affection. Adam told his sister, who said finally into a dish towel and then asked practical questions about holidays.

The keys stayed. The labels never happened. What changed was the sentence at the door: not water the plants, but I am home, said to a person who already had the means to be there. Seattle kept raining. The plants lived. Leah and Adam, who had practiced care in the small denomination of a spare key, discovered the large bill had been in their pockets the whole time. They spent it on purpose. The counter is still where they drop the metal at night, a sound both of them like, because it means the other one is in.

**End.**
