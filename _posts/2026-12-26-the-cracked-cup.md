The crack ran from the lip to the foot, a clean line, the kind that makes a person think of repair instead of the bin. Victor set the cup on Alma's counter. He was forty-nine. She was fifty-two and had clay on her cheek in a print she had stopped noticing.

"It was my father's," he said. "He is alive. He just does not drink from it anymore, and I do, and I am clumsy. I am Victor. If it cannot be saved, tell me quickly. I will be an adult about it."

"Alma. It can be saved. It will have a gold line where the crack was, if you want the repair visible, or a clay line if you want to pretend. I recommend visible. Pretending is how cups get dropped twice."

"Visible," Victor said. "I am done with pretending in several areas. The cup can start the trend."

She mended it in a week. He came back and held it up to the window. The gold was a thin honesty. He paid what she asked and then stood in the shop as if the transaction had not finished him.

"I would like a cup that was never broken," he said. "Made by you, used by me, with no father attached and no lesson. I will come drink from it if you have a chair. If the chair is a bridge too far, sell me the cup and I will leave like a customer."

"The chair is not too far," Alma said. "Thursday. I close at four. You can be the first person who is not a shipment."

Thursday he was early and waited in the car so he would not be a pressure at the door. She liked him for the car. They drank from new cups, a pair she had made between other orders, slightly uneven, clearly hers. Victor talked about a hardware store he managed and a move he had made to be nearer the father, who was fine and did not need a martyr. Alma talked about a daughter in Albuquerque and a kiln that was the most reliable relationship she had had in years. He asked about the kiln with real interest. She asked about the father with the same. Neither of them was a project.

The cracked cup sits on Victor's shelf, gold line catching the morning. The new cups sit on a table in the house they now share, and they are chipped in ordinary ways, from use, not from a story. Alma still recommends visible repairs. Victor still agrees. When something breaks, they put a line through it and keep the object. It is a method they like. It is also, in the best weeks, unnecessary, because the thing in their hands was made for them and has not had to survive a fall.
