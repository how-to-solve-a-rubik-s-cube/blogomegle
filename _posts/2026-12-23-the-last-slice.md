The oven was off. Chiara had said so with her eyebrows to three people already. Paolo was the fourth, and he saw the eyebrows and slowed down, which was why she did not point him to the street.

"One slice," he said. "I know the hour. I am Paolo. I will eat it on the step so you can lock the chairs. If the slice is already promised to the staff, I will be a rumor and leave."

"The slice is not promised," Chiara said. "It is the one I was going to eat standing up, which is a grim little tradition. Sit inside for ten minutes. Then I lock the door. I am Chiara. Twenty-five. The ten minutes are real."

"Thirty-three. I can eat at the speed of a closing."

He could. They shared the slice because grim traditions should be interrupted, and because it was large. The pizzeria at that hour was a cave of wiped tables and one light. Paolo was a paramedic coming off a shift that had been ordinary and therefore a success. Chiara was taking over the books from an uncle who trusted her and showed it by going home. She did not tell a story about the neighborhood. She lived in it. So did he. That was already a lot.

At ten minutes she stood. He stood with her, no negotiation. The chairs went up. The door locked. On the sidewalk the city was still awake in pockets.

"I am here on Thursdays," Paolo said. "Not as a last slice. As a person who would like twelve minutes at a reasonable hour. If ten was the correct amount of me, you can keep the tradition."

"Twelve on a Thursday," Chiara said. "Before eleven. Bring a friend if you want this to be casual. Bring only yourself if you do not."

He brought only himself. They took a table by the window while the oven was still on and other people were in the room, which kept the hour honest. Twelve minutes became a meal. The uncle met him and was less charming than Chiara, and Paolo took the less charming version as the real test and passed it by not trying to be a son. Chiara noticed. She notices most things. It is why the books balance.

They are past the sidewalk now. The last slice is still a joke they eat once a month after close, ten minutes, phones face down, then the lock. The rest of their time is not stolen from a shift. It is scheduled, like a person who has learned that showing up before the oven is off is a form of respect. Paolo keeps that respect. Chiara keeps the key. Between them the day has a shape, and the shape includes a light left on until both of them have eaten.

**End.**
