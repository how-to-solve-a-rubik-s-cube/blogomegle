The table had a scratch in the shape of a river, from a pan set down too fast years ago. Clara had stopped minding it. She was forty-eight. Edward was fifty, and he was cutting bread as if the cutting were part of the conversation, which it was. The window faced west. The light was doing the thing it does in every city at the end of a clear day, going gold, making even the sink look briefly ceremonial.

They had met in an ordinary way. A borrowed pen. A second day. A long stretch of choosing that did not need to be told again in order to be true. On this evening the story was the salad, the neighbor's radio, the bill that had been paid, the shoes by the door. Clara had had a meeting that ran long. Edward had had a delivery that came early. They reported these facts and were interested, which is a form of love that does not get enough songs.

"Stay there," Clara said, when he stood to clear. "The light is on your face. I am not making a portrait. I am noticing. Then you can clear."

He stayed. The gold lasted a minute. They let it. Then he cleared, because the noticing had been honest and the dishes were also honest, and a life that can hold both is the life they had built on purpose. Clara dried. Their shoulders touched and did not need to comment. The radio next door changed songs. Neither of them went to see what the new one was.

People ask them, sometimes, for the secret, as if a table were a trick. Edward says they eat at the same time when they can. Clara says they do not save the important sentence for a better room. Both answers are true and incomplete. The rest is this: when the light goes gold, someone notices, and when the minute is over, someone does the dishes, and neither task is assigned by gender or by grandeur. They are assigned by whoever is closest to the towel.

The hundredth evening is not different in kind from the tenth. That is the good news. Clara puts the bread away. Edward wipes the scratch of a river and does not try to sand it out. They sit again, because the chairs are comfortable and the day has one more cup in it. Outside, the city you know is turning its lights on, one window and then another. Inside, two adults are still at the table. Nothing is announced. Nothing needs to be. The gold is gone and the ordinary bulb is enough, and they are still there, which is the whole of the ending, and the whole of the beginning that kept.

**End.**
