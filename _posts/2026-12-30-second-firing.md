The first bowl folded in on itself with a soft sound that the whole class politely ignored. Carla sat back and put her hands in her lap. She was forty-two and had signed up for the class because her job, which was numbers for a hotel, had made her precise in ways that did not produce a single object she could hold. Rick was at the next wheel. He was forty-five. His bowl was also ugly. He looked over, not with pity.

"Mine has a wobble it is keeping as a pet," he said. "We can fire the failures and see if the kiln is kinder than we are. I am Rick."

"Carla. Fire them. I want evidence that I tried, even if the evidence is a pancake."

The pancake came out of the kiln looking like a decision. They laughed over it in the parking lot, two adults with paper-wrapped clay, and the laugh did the work that a better bowl would have claimed. Rick ran a landscaping crew. Carla had moved to Sedona for the job and stayed for the light, a reason she offered without decorating it. He offered a truck, a son who visited in summer, and a respect for people who retried things. They agreed to come early to the next class and center the clay before anyone was watching.

The second bowl held. It was thick in one place and thin in another and it existed. Carla glazed it a plain cream. Rick glazed his the same, by accident of a shared bucket, and they decided the matching was funny enough to keep. After the second firing they went for a drink that was mostly water, because both of them had early mornings, and they put the bowls on the table like small animals that had survived a storm.

"I would like to see you when we are not covered in slip," Rick said. "A hike, a short one, the kind that ends at a diner. If the class is the right size of us, we can leave it at the kiln."

"The hike," Carla said. "Then the diner. I want to know who you are when nothing is collapsing."

Nothing collapsed. The hike was hot and ordinary and good. The diner had pie. Rick did not become a different man away from the wheel, which was the news Carla needed. She did not become easier, only more visible. They kept the class through the session and then kept the Sunday morning, which was the slot the class had vacated. The first bowl, the pancake, sits on a shelf in the house they share, unglazed on one side, a record. The second bowls are used for fruit. Carla likes the order. Failure first, kept on purpose. Then the thing that holds.

**End.**
