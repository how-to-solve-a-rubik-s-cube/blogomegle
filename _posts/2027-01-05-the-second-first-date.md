They had already done the hard conversation, months ago, in a parked car, where Neil had told the truth about a relapse and Janet had told the truth about a fear she would not manage for him. He was forty. She was thirty-seven. They had parted as two adults who were not ready, which is a less famous ending than a fight and a better one. In the spring he asked, by email, whether dinner could be a first date, not a continuation. She wrote back that a first date was allowed if nobody brought a speech.

The restaurant was loud enough to keep them from being solemn. Neil wore a shirt that fit. Janet ordered the fish and did not interview him about meetings. He did not interview her about whether she had waited. They talked about a trail he had hiked and a class she was teaching and a nephew who had opinions about dinosaurs. It was almost boring. Janet felt her shoulders drop and realized she had come armed for a different evening.

"This is what I wanted," she said, over the plates. "A Tuesday that does not refer to the car. We can refer to it if we need to. We do not need to tonight."

"Tonight is the fish," Neil said. "And the fact that I am glad to see you and I went to a meeting this morning and I am not offering the meeting as a ticket. I am offering the next dinner, if the first date is allowed to have a sequel that is also ordinary."

"The sequel is allowed," Janet said. "Saturday. Something with walking. I do not want a booth to be the only room we know."

They walked on Saturday. The ordinary held. It is not a small achievement. Anyone can be interesting in a crisis. Neil kept being a person who showed up on time and told the truth in amounts she could use. Janet kept being a person who did not run a rescue. When the hard subject returned, as hard subjects do, they sat at her table and had it, and then they finished making the salad. The sequence mattered. The life was not the crisis with meals around it. The meals were the life, and the crisis was a thing they knew how to put down.

They still call that dinner the second first date, because the first first dates had belonged to other years and other people. This one is theirs. Denver is bright in the evening. The fish was slightly too salty. Neither of them has improved the story by forgetting that. A good beginning can be ordinary and still be the one you stay for.

**End.**
