They wrote it on a calendar in the diner where they had met, on a Thursday, in ink. Nancy was forty-five. Glenn was forty-seven. The agreement was one evening a week, held, for a year, unless someone was ill or out of town, in which case the evening moved and did not vanish. No proposal. No talk of proposals. The rule was Glenn's suggestion and Nancy's relief. She had done a marriage that treated every month as a progress report. He had done a relationship that was only intensity. Both of them wanted a unit of time they could actually keep.

Thursday was soup or a film or a walk, and once, memorably, an argument about a paint color that they finished and then went to bed on time. They did not miss a week. In March, Nancy had the flu and Glenn sat in her hallway and read the paper on the other side of the door, which was theatrical only if you did not know he left after twenty minutes because she asked him to. He left. The Thursday counted.

Friends asked what it was building toward. Nancy said it was building toward December. Glenn said December was a Thursday like the others, plus cake if she wanted cake. She wanted cake. They ate it in the diner. The calendar page was full. Nobody got down on a knee. The waitress, who had watched the year, brought two forks and did not wink.

"Next year," Nancy said, "I would like the same calendar. I would also like a key. The key is not a staircase. It is a key. I am saying the size of the want."

"The key, and the calendar," Glenn said. "If a Thursday needs to become a Wednesday once, we will survive. Forty-seven. I am not waiting for the year to turn into a different kind of proof. This was the proof."

She gave him the key in January. He used it on Thursdays and, later, on a Tuesday when her car would not start, and the Tuesday did not rewrite the pact. It joined it. They still do not talk about proposals. They talk about August, which is crowded, and about a trip that has to be scheduled around the evening they will not give up. Fifty-two weeks is not a small thing. It is a life with a spine. Nancy feels it when she is tired. Glenn feels it when he is offered a plan that would erase a Thursday and he says no without a speech. The diner still has the corner. The ink on the first calendar has faded. The habit has not. That is the ending they wanted. It does not need a better one.

**End.**
