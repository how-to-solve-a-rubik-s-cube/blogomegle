The meeting was about a shared wall between a bookshop and a gallery, and about who would pay to fix the damp. Erin represented the bookshop. She was thirty-six and had a folder. Patrick represented the gallery. He was thirty-nine and had a different folder. The cafe had put them at a table too small for folders, which was the first useful thing it did.

They argued the damp for twenty minutes with civility and no progress. The coffee went cold. Erin drank hers anyway and made a face.

"This is awful," she said. "Not the terms. The coffee. I am going to order another and also say the thing under the terms. Your gallery uses our doorway as a lobby and I am tired of pretending the doorway is a philosophical question. It is a door."

"It is a door," Patrick said. "We should pay for the closer and half the damp. I have been stalling because my board likes a stall. I am more interested in a second coffee than in their pace. If I say that, I am Patrick, not a folder. Thirty-nine."

"Erin. Thirty-six. Order the coffee. Then we will write the half on a napkin before you become a folder again."

They wrote it. The napkin was later typed into an email both boards could tolerate, but the napkin was the true document. With the second cup, which was hot, they stayed past the repair. Erin talked about a father who still sent her clippings about the death of retail. Patrick talked about a sister who thought galleries were a tax on the sincere. They compared the clippings and the sister and did not try to solve either. The cafe filled and emptied. A server took the folders off the spare chair so a stranger could sit, and they let the folders go to the floor, a small abdication.

"This was not a date," Erin said. "If we want a date, we should call it one and put it on a different day, so the damp does not get to be our chaperone."

"Thursday," Patrick said. "No folders. There is a place that does not serve this coffee, which is now a requirement."

Thursday had no folders. The wall got its repair in the following month, paid as agreed. Erin and Patrick got a habit of Thursday and a disagreement about a novel that lasted longer than the damp and was more enjoyable. The bookshop and the gallery still share a building. The staff have theories. The theories are less accurate than the napkin, which Patrick keeps in a drawer at home, not as a relic, as a reminder that a cold cup was the moment he stopped speaking for a board. Erin keeps the typed email. Between the two versions of the truth, they built a third: a table with nothing under it but their own feet, and a door that closes properly, on a closer they both paid for, in the proportions they chose.

**End.**
