The porch had been a project for longer than the wood deserved. Joel was forty-four and a carpenter who had started the job in September. Denise was forty-one and the owner, and she had approved each delay because the delays came with his presence, which she had not put in the estimate. By November the floor was sound and the railing was a theory they discussed over coffee he was no longer billing.

"I am padding the work," Joel said, one morning, because he was decent. "There is one rotten board left. I can finish it today. If I come back after that, it should not be with a saw. I would like to come back. I am saying so while I still have a tool in my hand, so you can hear that I know the difference."

Denise looked at the board. It was actually rotten. The rest of the excuse was not.

"Finish it today," she said. "Come back Friday with no tools. I will make the coffee. If we are boring without a project, we should know before the railing becomes a metaphor we have to live under. Forty-one. I have done metaphors. I want a porch and a person, labeled correctly."

"Forty-four. Friday. No tools."

He finished the board by three. The railing went in by four, because it had been ready for a week. On Friday he came with a plant instead of a saw, which was a risk, and she laughed and put the plant on the step that could finally hold it. They sat on the new floor and talked about a son of his who lived with an ex and a job of hers in city permits, a job that had made her suspicious of vague timelines. He offered a timeline: dinner the next week, a walk, a month of seeing whether the coffee held. She accepted the month and added a clause. If either of them wanted to stop, they would say stop, not suddenly discover a conflict with a subcontractor.

The month held. The porch held. In the spring they replaced a single nail that had popped, a real repair, billed at the price of zero because he lived there by then and the house was a shared set of problems. Denise still works in permits. Joel still builds other people's stairs. Their own stairs are done. The unfinished part of the story closed when they stopped using lumber as a reason to stand next to each other. The reason is ordinary now. It does not need a saw. It needs Friday, and it gets it.

**End.**
