The smoke alarm was accurate and unkind. Paige stood on a chair and waved a magazine at it while the tart cooled into a photograph of failure. She was twenty-nine. The top was black. The custard underneath, she suspected, had survived out of spite. Someone knocked during the third wave of the magazine.

Nick lived across the landing. He was thirty-four and held a glass of water as if water were a plan.

"I am not the fire department," he said. "I am Nick. I have a window that opens better than yours. If you want it, bring the tart. If you want me to leave, say so over the alarm."

"Paige. Bring the window. I am not dying. I am baking, which is worse, because I chose it."

They put the tart on his sill, where the night air dealt with it. The alarm stopped. In the quiet they looked at the damage and then at each other, and Paige laughed first, which gave him permission. He found a knife. The black lid came off in sheets. The center was, improbably, edible. They ate it standing at the counter with two forks and no plates, a method Paige called field conditions.

"I was trying to impress a sister who is not even in town until Sunday," she said. "I practice disasters. Twenty-nine. You have now seen the rehearsal."

"The rehearsal was good," Nick said. "The tart is better than my pride would be in your place. I install lighting. I have burned wires. We are colleagues in small ruins. Thirty-four."

Sunday the sister came and the second tart was only brown at the edge. Nick was invited for a slice and a boundary: he could stay an hour, and he would be introduced as the neighbor, not as a plot. He stayed fifty minutes, praised the crust, and left while the evening was still good. Paige walked him to the landing.

"Come back when there is no audience," she said. "I make a better omelet than I make a tart. That is a fact, not a lure. You can test it."

He tested it on a Tuesday. The omelet was better. His lighting in her kitchen was a later project, paid for properly, because she would not take a favor that looked like a debt. He liked the invoice. It meant she expected him to still be around when it came due, as a neighbor and as a man. The smoke alarm has a new battery he installed and she recorded. The burnt tart is a story they tell without the magazine. What they kept was the knock, the window, and a woman who invited him back when no one needed impressing.

**End.**
