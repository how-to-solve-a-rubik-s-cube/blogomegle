The belt stopped. A light flashed. Nobody's clothes appeared. Giulia watched the empty slats go around once more, as if persistence were a technology. She was forty-six. Marco stood a polite distance away, watching the same nothing. He was forty-nine. Their flights had landed within twenty minutes of each other, which meant nothing except that the airline could disappoint in bulk.

"If you are about to say it could be worse," Giulia said, "I will share my bad mood accurately."

"I was about to say the form is at the desk and the line is already a novel," Marco said. "I am Marco. I can hold a place. I cannot conjure a suitcase."

"Giulia. Hold the place. I will conjure the forms."

They filled them out shoulder to shoulder, the way strangers do when a counter demands it. Her bag was a gray one with a red strap. His was black and, he admitted, identical to half the city. The agent gave them the same speech. They stepped away with reference numbers and no faith.

"I live here," Giulia said. "The bag has my mother's dishes in it, wrapped badly, which I will not forgive myself for if they break. I am going to eat anyway. There is a trattoria that will serve me even if I look like a person who has lost a fight with a belt. If you are stranded, come. If you are not, you have been adequate company, which is a real compliment today."

"I am stranded until morning," Marco said. "I accept."

The trattoria knew her. The waiter brought wine before they asked and did not ask why a stranger was at her table. Marco was in Rome for a contract, three months of engineering on a station roof, and he had the careful cheer of a man trying not to resent a city before he had walked it. Giulia restored ceramics and had the forearms to prove it. She talked about the dishes. He did not say they were only things. He said he hoped the wrapping held, and if it did not, he knew a crate from work that could carry the survivors home. She looked at him then, properly, and decided the evening was better than the belt.

The bags came at noon the next day. The dishes had a chip in one rim and were otherwise themselves. Giulia sent Marco a photograph of the chip and the address of the trattoria again. He came after his shift on the roof, dust in his hair, and she let the dust be part of the picture. Three months was a container they did not pretend was infinite. They used it. At the end of the contract he stayed for a reason with a start date and a union form, not for a speech at an airport. The carousel still eats people's weeks. Theirs gave back a chipped bowl they keep on a shelf, a reminder that the loss was never the point. The point was a woman who invited a stranded man to dinner and a man who showed up after the luggage had already been found.
