Tuesday began as a class. Susan took it because her knees liked the movement and her weeks liked a landmark. She was fifty-one. Richard was in the same row because his doctor had been specific and his pride had finally been tired. He was fifty-four. They nodded, then talked, then went for soup after, a soup that was not a reward for exercise so much as a reason to stretch the evening by an hour.

The class ended in May. The soup did not. They kept Tuesday anyway, a decision made in the parking lot without a speech. Susan said, "I am not ready to only see you by accident." Richard said, "Then Tuesday is on purpose." They put it in their phones under the word soup, which was accurate and also private.

Through the summer the soup became a walk when the evenings were long, and the walk became a film when the rain returned, and the film became a key. Nobody renovated a house on camera. Susan's house gained a second toothbrush and a chair that was clearly his, because he sat in it. Richard's apartment stayed his for a while, a room he used for the early mornings and the quiet, and she did not treat the room as a threat. He told her when he was ready to let it go. She helped him pack on a Sunday, which was not Tuesday, and the exception was noted and allowed.

People at the class reunion asked if they were serious. Susan said they were Tuesdays. Richard added that Tuesdays had turned out to contain most of a life, if you kept them. The line got a laugh and was also just true. They have a garden now that is mostly his project and her approval, and a winter routine of the same soup place, where the staff do not congratulate them. That lack of applause is one of the things they love about it. A performance would have frozen them in the first chapter, two people who met in a row of mats. Staying let them become people who argue about mulch and still show up at six.

The phones still say soup. The reminder is no longer necessary. They keep it because deleting it would feel like claiming they had outgrown the original promise. They have not outgrown it. They have built a house around it. Tuesday remains the beam.

**End.**
