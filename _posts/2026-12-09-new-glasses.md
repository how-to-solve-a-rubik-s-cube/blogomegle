Lars had chosen the frames three times. They were round, dark, and slightly too serious for his face, which was not a serious face until he was concentrating. Sigrid had let it go the first time because taste is allowed to be wrong. The second time she had winced and stayed professional. The third time she put the frames on the tray and folded her hands.

"I am going to be a person instead of a shop," she said. "Those make you look like you are about to deliver bad news about a fjord. Try the other pair. I am Sigrid. Forty-five. You can ignore me and still buy the tragic ones."

Lars laughed, which improved the tragic ones and confirmed her point. He was forty-eight. He tried the other pair. The mirror became kinder.

"I keep choosing the first pair because my brother said they were intellectual," he said. "My brother is not invited to my face. I will take the kind ones. And I will take the correction, if it comes with your name, which it did."

She adjusted the kind ones herself. Her fingers at his temples were a job and also not, and both of them felt the difference and did not make it a scene in a shop full of people squinting. Lars paid. At the door he turned.

"I write manuals for a software company," he said. "I am free at six. If the person who told me the truth wants a coffee that is not sold beside lens cloths, I will be at the place on the corner. If she wants to remain a shop, I will wear the kind glasses either way."

Sigrid went at six-ten, which was her version of punctuality. The glasses were right. She told him so. He told her the manuals were dull and the people in his office were not, and that he had been squinting for a year because he did not want to admit the world had gone soft at the edges. She said admission was cheaper than a headache. They talked until the corner place started stacking chairs.

The next pair, a year later, he chose without his brother and with her, on a Saturday when she was not working and the other optician pretended not to smirk. Lars did not move into the shop. Sigrid did not rewrite his manuals. They did move into a flat with good lamps, because both of them had become evangelists for seeing what is there. He still offers her the tragic frames as a joke, once a year, and she still takes them off his face with two fingers. The truth, repeated, turned out to be a durable affection. He looks like himself. She is the person who insisted on it.
