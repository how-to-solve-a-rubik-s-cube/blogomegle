The lease was on the kitchen table under a mug that had left a ring. Jordan moved the mug and read the new rent twice. She was twenty-six. Sam was twenty-seven and had been her roommate for two years of compatible chores and one year, more recently, of a feeling neither of them had put in a shared document.

"We can sign, or we can pretend the city is full of other apartments," Jordan said. "I would like to say the other thing first. I do not want to be your roommate who is in love with you. I want the love to be in the lease, or I want a different apartment. I am not threatening you. I am refusing a fog."

Sam sat down. The radiator knocked, which gave him a second to be as brave as she had been.

"I have been using the chores as a place to hide," he said. "The hiding is over. I want the lease. I want it with you, named correctly. If we sign and we are bad at the naming, we will talk before we are silent. That is my whole counteroffer."

"Accepted," Jordan said. "We tell the friends in plain words. We do not let them guess and then punish us for the guess."

They told the friends. Some of them had a pool going, which Jordan found rude and accurate. The landlord got a boring email. The apartment got a small rearrangement: his coat moved to a hook that was not a guest hook, her plant moved to the sill that actually got light, the lease scanned and saved in both of their accounts.

The first month was not a honeymoon. It was a negotiation about dishes that had been easier when love was a rumor. They kept the rule. They talked before they were silent. Sam still worked early at a bakery. Jordan still worked late at a clinic desk. The overlap was breakfast on one side and a note on the other, and the note was better than it had been, because it could say miss you without pretending it meant nothing.

They renewed again the next year, not because a lease is romantic, because a lease is a date you have to mean. Jordan is thirty now in the life after this story's first page, and Sam is thirty-one, but on the day they signed they were twenty-six and twenty-seven and shaking only a little. The mug still leaves rings. They put it on a coaster when they remember. The rest of the promise they remember on purpose.
