The laundry room was at the end of the hall and smelled like warm dust. Maya was twenty-eight and did her wash on Sundays because Saturdays were for the market job and her own life. Theo was thirty-one and had the same idea, which meant they had been nodding for months, each of them guarding a machine.

The dryer ate a sock in March. It was blue, and it was Maya's, and she knew it was gone because she owned three pairs and counted. She taped a note to the machine: If you open this and a blue sock escapes, I am in 4C. No reward except my thanks and the sock.

Theo found it in April, tucked under the lint screen he had finally pulled all the way out. He washed it, because returning a dirty sock felt like a bit. He knocked on 4C with the sock in a paper bag, which he realized mid-knock was an odd presentation.

"I am Theo, from 4F," he said. "Thirty-one. Your sock survived. I washed it. This is not a courtship. It is a return."

"Maya. Twenty-eight. A washed sock is a courtship in this building. Come in or the hallway will invent a version."

He came in as far as the kitchen, which was also the living room. The sock was undeniably hers. She made tea because the bag had created a social debt and because she wanted to look at a man who cleaned a lint screen all the way. Theo told her he worked at a bike shop and lived with a cactus he was not killing. Maya told him she managed the market's bookstall and lived with a sister three nights a week, so the apartment would sometimes have a second voice and he should not be startled.

They started sharing the Sunday machines on purpose, one load after the other, so neither of them had to hover in the hall. Someone brought quarters. Someone brought a playlist that was not embarrassing, a higher bar than it sounds. The dryer behaved. They almost missed having a quest.

In May the sister was away and Maya asked Theo up after the last load, not for a speech, for pasta. He brought a lemon. They ate with the window open onto a noisy street and did not pretend the street was a soundtrack. Theo washed the pot. Maya dried. It was not the laundry room. It was adjacent to the idea of it, a chore done beside a person, which had been the original pleasure.

They still do Sunday wash. The sock is back in the pair. Theo has a key now, given after a summer of knocking, and he still knocks when the sister is home, because a key is not a permission to skip a person. Maya loves him for the knock as much as for the lint screen. The hall is the same hall. The end of it is a room that used to be only machines and is now, also, the place they met, which they pass with bags of clothes and the ordinary pride of a plan that kept happening.

**End.**
