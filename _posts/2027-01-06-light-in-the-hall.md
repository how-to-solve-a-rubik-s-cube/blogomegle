The hall light was a small yellow that did not pretend to be a sunrise. Elspeth left it on when Iain worked the late shift at the station bookstall, a retirement job he loved more than the career it had followed. She was sixty-six. He was sixty-three. They had shared the flat for three years and had never had a dramatic chapter, a fact they mentioned with pride when other people brought them dramas as if dramas were gifts.

The light was for the stairs, which were narrow, and for the pleasure of arriving to a sign that a person had thought of the arrival. Iain came in quietly and turned it off. In the kitchen a plate waited under another, smaller light, also her doing. He ate. She was sometimes awake and sometimes not. When she was awake they talked about the late customers, who were often kinder than the daytime ones. When she was asleep he washed the plate and set the table for a morning that would include both of them.

One night the bulb failed. He came home to a dark stair and a moment of unreasonable loss. He said so at breakfast, not as a complaint about her, as a report from a man who had discovered the size of a habit.

"I will buy the bulbs in pairs," Elspeth said. "And you will tell me when the late shift changes, so the light is a plan and not a superstition. I like you. I like being the person who thinks of the stair. I intend to keep the job."

"It is the best job in the house," Iain said. "I will keep coming home to it. If I am ever earlier than the light, I will turn it on for you when you come back from your sister's. Fair is fair."

They did that. The sister lived across town, and the visits ran late, and Elspeth started to find the yellow waiting, and the pleasure was exactly the same from the other side. Nobody wrote a speech about it. The bulbs were a standing item on the grocery list, between oats and stamps. Friends who wanted a bigger story were told the truth: this is the story. A lit stair, a plate, a man who washes up, a woman who remembers the spare bulb. A life can be made of that and be full. Theirs is. The hall is short. The light is ordinary. The coming home is the part they refuse to treat as small, and so it stays large enough to live inside.

**End.**
