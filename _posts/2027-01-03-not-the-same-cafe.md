The old cafe had their breakup in the walls, or at least in the table by the window where Jonas had said he needed a year and Maren had said a year was a way of leaving without a verb. They were younger then by enough. She was thirty-two now. He was thirty-five. The year had happened. He had not used it as a trick. He had gone to a job in Hamburg and come back when the job ended, and he had written first, a short note that did not ask for the window table.

They chose a place neither of them had been. The chairs were plastic. The coffee was honest. A fluorescent tube buzzed and nobody planned to photograph it. Maren put her bag on the spare seat and looked at him without the old script.

"We can be new here," she said. "If you start a sentence with back then, I will stop you. I want to know whether you can sit in an ugly room and want the person in front of you, not the person you remember."

"I can," Jonas said. "The person in front of me pays her own coffee and is better at boundaries than the one I left. I am better at verbs. I am back. I am asking for a second ugly coffee next week, not for a reconstruction."

They had the second coffee. Then a walk that avoided a certain street on purpose, which was not denial, it was design. Maren had changed offices. Jonas had changed the way he left a room: he said when he would return, and then he returned at that time. The fluorescent cafe became a Thursday. The staff learned their order and did not learn their history, a privacy they tipped for.

Friends asked whether they were repeating a mistake. Maren said the mistake had been a vague year, and the current arrangement was a calendar. Jonas agreed in front of the friends, not later. That public agreement mattered more to her than the cafe. A person who will claim you in a bad light is a person you can take into a good one.

They eventually moved to a third cafe, because the plastic chairs finally defeated them, and the third one is only slightly less ugly. The point survived the move. They do not need the original room. They need a room that does not belong to the old sentence. In it, they talk about work and a borrowed drill and a weekend plan that has an end time and a beginning. Staying, for them, is not a dramatic return. It is a Thursday they keep in a place that never saw them fail, until the place is no longer the proof. They are.

**End.**
