The diner kept the lights low enough that scrubs did not look like a costume. Naomi slid into a booth because her feet had filed a complaint. She was forty, an internist, and she had promised herself eggs before she reentered the civilization of her apartment. Isaac was in the next booth, already eating, forty-six, a pediatrician who had the same promise going and was further along.

A toddler two tables over sneezed with conviction. Both of them glanced, assessed, and relaxed. The glance collided. Isaac huffed a laugh.

"We are off the clock," he said. "I am going to say that out loud so neither of us starts a clinic in a booth. I am Isaac. The eggs here are better than the coffee. That is my whole medical opinion."

"Naomi. Then I will order the eggs and not the case history. Sit here if you want a table that is not a chart. If you want silence, I am very good at it."

He wanted the table. They traded first names and specialties and then, by agreement, stopped trading hospital. Naomi had a garden that was mostly mint because mint forgave her schedule. Isaac had a dog that was mostly sofa. The ordinary details did more work than a confession would have. She was not looking for someone to debrief. She was looking for someone who understood why she was eating at ten and would still ask about the mint.

"I do this booth on Thursdays," Isaac said, at the end. "Not as a standing appointment. As a fact. If you are ever a fact here too, bring a story about the garden and not about the ward. I have a ward. I come here to put it down."

"I can put mine down," Naomi said. "Thursday. Forty. I will be the one with the better posture and the worse coffee habit."

She was there the next Thursday, posture as advertised. They built a rule and kept it: no cases, no colleagues, no cleverness about suffering. The rule made the rest possible. Isaac met the mint and was properly impressed by its will to live. Naomi met the dog and was accepted after a probationary sniff. Their schedules remained a puzzle. They solved it the way they solved other hard things, with a written plan and a willingness to revise.

Nobody at the hospital owns this story. They did not meet over a bed. They met over eggs, after the work, with the work set on a chair they refused to let speak. It is the cleanest beginning either of them has had, and they protect it. The diner knows their order. The garden is more than mint now. The dog has learned a second set of footsteps and thumps the sofa in welcome, which is as clinical an outcome as anyone needs.

**End.**
