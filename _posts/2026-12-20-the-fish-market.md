Luc had said half a kilo and the vendor had heard optimism. He stood outside the market with a bag that was ethically and practically too much fish for a man who lived alone and owned one pan. He was forty-seven. Sophie was at the next stall, buying lemons with the focus of a person who had a list and a limit. She was forty-four.

"You are going to apologize to that bag all the way home," she said. "I am Sophie. I am not offering to save you. I am observing."

"Luc. The observation is correct. I cook adequately and in small amounts. This is a crowd."

She could have walked. She had a morning. She also had a kitchen that could handle a crowd, and a curiosity about whether he would hand the problem to the first woman who named it. He did not. He said, "If you know a way to cook it and you want half, I will carry the bag and leave when the half is in your pan. If you do not want a man in your kitchen, I will find a neighbor and a recipe, in that order."

"I want the fish," Sophie said. "I will decide about the man after I see whether he washes a board."

He washed the board. He also washed the lemons without being told they had market on them. Sophie's kitchen was above a street that argued happily with itself. She salted the fish and did not narrate. Luc did the onions. They worked as if they had negotiated it, which they had, in one sentence at the door. Half the fish went into her pan. Half went into a container for him, with a written time and temperature, because she was a person who wrote things down. She taught at a maritime school. He ran a chandlery. The harbor was in both their days and had not, until now, put them in a room.

"Eat yours with bread," she said. "Come back for the pan another day if the instructions fail. Do not come back tonight. Tonight I have a class plan and a limit."

"Tonight was never the request," Luc said. "Thursday is a day I can return a container. Forty-seven. I will knock."

He knocked on Thursday with the container clean and a lemon that was not required. Sophie let him in because the fish had been good and the board had been washed and the limit had been respected. They ate the leftover plan, which was soup. It took longer than soup. Luc did not move the salt cellar to a more logical place. Sophie noticed every un-moved thing and relaxed by degrees.

They are at the market on Saturdays now, with a list and a limit, buying one correct amount. The extra bag remains their origin myth, told with the part where she was not obliged. That part is why she opens the door. A man who can carry too much fish home without making it a woman's work is a man who can be invited into a kitchen on Thursday, and then, eventually, on days with no container to return.

**End.**
