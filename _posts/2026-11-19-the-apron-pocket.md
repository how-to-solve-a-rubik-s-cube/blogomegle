Mara kept the card in the apron pocket because Ellis kept forgetting it.

He was thirty-seven, an accountant who could balance a year of someone else's money and still leave his wallet under a stack of invoices. She was thirty-four and had been up since four. By the time he arrived, the morning buns were gone and the rye was still warm enough to fog the bag.

"I had it," he said, which was what he always said.

"You had it last Tuesday," Mara said. "Today you have a receipt and a hopeful face."

The shop sat on a Portland corner where the rain sounded like someone sorting rice. Ellis stepped aside for a woman buying six loaves for an office she did not like. Mara watched him do it and decided he was not performing. Some people move because they have been told to. He moved because the woman was carrying too much.

She put the rye in a bag and wrote his name on the tape. It was unnecessary. She did it anyway.

The card turned up on Thursday, in the apron, behind a pencil and a bent paperclip. Mara could have charged the loaf and forgotten the rest. She set the card on the register with a note instead. Ellis. You left this. The rye is not a gift. Come pay, and stay long enough to eat the end piece. I am thirty-four. I close at two on Fridays.

He came at one-forty. He paid. He ate the end piece standing up, because the one chair was occupied by a delivery driver. They talked about a tax season that had made him unkind to his own lunch, and about a starter she had kept alive for nine years, which was longer than her last lease.

"I am not late on purpose," he said.

"I know," Mara said. "You are late the way the oven is late. It gets there."

They made a rule that was not romantic and then was. Fridays, one-forty, the end piece, the card in his hand before he ordered. If he forgot it, he did not get to pretend the forgetting was charming. He got to walk to the bank and come back. He did that once. He came back wet and on time for the second arrival, which Mara counted as the better one.

A year later the card lives in the front of his wallet, where a person can find it without a search. Mara still writes his name on the tape. She does not need to. She likes the look of it. What lasted was not the lost card. It was a Friday that no longer depended on one, and a man who learned where his own pocket was because someone noticed he was trying.
