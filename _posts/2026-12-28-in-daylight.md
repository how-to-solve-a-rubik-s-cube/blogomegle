The chats had all been after eleven, which was when Kate's shift ended and when Evan's insomnia pretended to be a personality. She was twenty-nine. He was thirty-three. The app was one of the ones that pairs strangers for a conversation and then forgets them. They had not let it forget. They had traded numbers with the specific rule that daylight would be the test. If the other person was dull at noon, they would say so and part without a tragedy.

They chose a lake path because it was public and free and would not smell like a bar. Evan arrived looking like a person who owned a coat. Kate arrived looking like herself, which was the point. For a minute the night conversations hovered at the edge of the path, embarrassed.

"You are taller," Kate said. "And you have a scar through your eyebrow you did not mention. I like the scar. I am not starting with a soul. I am starting with a face. Hello in the actual light."

"Hello," Evan said. "You laugh the same. That was the part I did not want to lose. The rest I was prepared to renegotiate. I brought a worse version of my night personality. It is called Wednesday."

Wednesday was good. They walked. Evan worked in a warehouse office and was studying, slowly, for a logistics certificate. Kate dispatched vans and had a clear eye for a bad route, which she demonstrated on a man who almost biked into them. They ate sandwiches on a bench. There was no music. There was a great deal of ordinary information: a mother, a roommate, a preference for mustard. The mustard mattered because it was not a metaphor. It was mustard.

"I was afraid you would evaporate," Kate said. "People are brave at midnight and administrative at noon."

"I am administrative," Evan said. "I also want to see you on a Saturday that has a plan. A market. A time. I will text it before Friday so it is not a mood."

The text came on Thursday, with a corner and an hour. He was there early. She was there on time. Nothing vanished except the idea that they were only a voice in the dark. They still talk at eleven sometimes, from the same sofa now, which is a pleasant corruption of the origin. The lake path remains a place they go when they need to remember the test and how they passed it. Daylight did not make them smaller. It made them checkable. Two adults stood still long enough to be seen, and then they kept the appointment. The night can have the rest. Noon was the part that counted.

**End.**
