The class was called Absolute Beginners, which Hamish appreciated because it was the rare room that did not ask him to pretend. He was sixty-nine. Moira was sixty-six and had signed up because her friend had canceled and the fee was already paid, a motive she announced so no one would call her brave.

"I will step on you," Hamish said, when the teacher paired them by proximity. "I am telling you now so the stepping is data and not a betrayal. I am Hamish."

"Moira. Step carefully, then. I have a knee with opinions. I also have nowhere else to be on a Tuesday, so you may use the hour."

He counted wrong. She could hear the music and he could hear a different music, slightly delayed, as if the band were in the next close. They laughed instead of correcting each other into shame. The teacher clapped a pattern. Hamish missed it. Moira squeezed his hand once, which meant try again, not you are failing. He tried again. The second time his foot went where she had left a space. It felt like a small engineering victory.

After class the hall smelled like dust and orange squash. Moira put her shoes in a bag. Hamish hovered with the look of a man who had prepared a sentence in the coat queue.

"There is a cafe that does not mind sweat," he said. "If you want a scone and a postmortem. If you want to escape, the door is the one you came in."

"A scone is not an escape," Moira said. "Lead on. Count if you must. I will ignore the count and follow the actual street."

They ate. Hamish had been a joiner. His hands were still nicked and still gentle on a cup. Moira had kept the books for a bus depot and missed the drivers more than the numbers. Both had buried people they had loved, and they put those names on the table early, the way you label a dish so no one is surprised. Then they talked about the class, which was easier and not lesser. He asked if she would request him next week, so the pairing would be a choice. She said she would request him, and that he would have to accept corrections about the beat.

He accepted them. By the fifth Tuesday he was only half a count behind. Moira's knee survived. They added a walk along the river on Sundays, no music, no teacher, just the pace they had learned by colliding softly. Hamish still steps wrong when he is excited. Moira still squeezes once. The class thinks they are sweet, which they tolerate. At home, in the flat they eventually shared, the sweetness is more practical: a hand at the elbow on the stair, a scone cut in half, a man who learned the beat because a woman asked him to, and a woman who stayed in reach while he learned.

**End.**
