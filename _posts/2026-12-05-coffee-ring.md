The map was a free one from the station, already wrong about a temple gate. Yuki had circled the wrong gate in pencil. She was thirty-two and on a day off from a conservation lab, still in the city she lived in, playing visitor because her own neighborhood had gone stale in her eyes. Kenji sat at the next table with a camera he was not using. He was thirty-five. A drop from his cup landed on her map and made a ring around a river.

"I have edited your plan," he said. "I am Kenji. I will buy you a new map or a better cup. Your choice."

"Yuki. Leave the ring. I was going to the wrong gate anyway. Thirty-two. If you have nowhere to be, be wrong with me for an hour. I do this alone too often and then pretend it is taste."

They followed the ring, which was not a direction, so they chose the river instead. Kyoto in late spring was full of people who wanted a photograph and a few who wanted a bench. Yuki and Kenji wanted the bench. He was a high school photography teacher on a Wednesday stolen back from grading. She cleaned pigments out of old screens and came home with hands that smelled like solvent. They compared the ways a job can make you careful until careful is the only speed you have.

The hour became three. They ate noodles standing up. Kenji did not take her picture. Yuki noticed and was grateful in a way she did not want to explain and then did explain, because unexplained gratitude turns into a test.

"People photograph me when I look content," she said. "It feels like a withdrawal from an account I did not open. Walk beside me instead."

"I can do that," Kenji said. "I am thirty-five. I have a class tomorrow at eight. If you want the wrong gate on Saturday, correctly this time, I will meet you at the river coffee. I will leave the camera at home."

He left it at home. She brought the stained map, for the joke, and they found the right gate and a garden that was not famous and therefore had seats. A month later the map was in a frame in her kitchen, coffee ring and all, which she had done without asking him, and when she showed him she was briefly afraid the gesture was too much. He looked at it and said, "Good. Now we can get lost with a clean map."

They do. The lab is still careful. The classroom is still early. Saturdays are less efficient on purpose. The ring on the paper is a record of a spill, not an omen, and they like it that way. Two adults decided a stain could stay, and then decided they could too.
