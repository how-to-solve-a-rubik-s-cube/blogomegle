The platform smelled like hot metal and oranges. The train was late, and the announcement admitted it without shame. Lucia had a bottle of water she had already opened. She was twenty-seven and dressed for an office that had air conditioning and a boss who thought heat was a character flaw. Mateo had no bottle. He was forty-six, in a work shirt, and he was doing the arithmetic of dignity, which was whether he could ask.

She held the bottle out before he finished the arithmetic.

"Take it," Lucia said. "I am getting off in two stops and I have another at home. I am Lucia. This is not a story about fate. It is hot."

"Mateo. Then I will drink like a person and not like a symbol. Thank you."

He drank a third and handed it back, wiping the mouth of the bottle with his shirt, a care that made her like him more than the thirst had. They did not pretend to have grown up in the same neighborhood. She was from Valencia and worked in scheduling for a hospital. He was from Madrid and installed elevators, a fact that made him look at the station stairs with professional sorrow. The train came, hotter than the platform. They stood near the door.

"I would like to return the water," Mateo said. "Not the same water. A cold bottle, and a coffee, at an hour the sun is less proud. There is a kiosk by Atocha that knows me. Tomorrow at seven in the evening. If you want. Forty-six, so the evening is not a surprise."

"Twenty-seven. Seven is a real hour. I will come if my shift ends. If it does not, I will not send a tragic message. I will come the next day."

The shift ended. She came. He had two bottles and the coffee and a small embarrassment about the excess, which she relieved by taking all of it. They walked until the streets widened. Mateo told her he had a teenage daughter who lived with him every other week and set the rules of the apartment, including no performances of romance in the kitchen. Lucia said that was a good rule for a kitchen and that she had no interest in being a performance. She told him about a sister and a night class. He listened as if scheduling were a respectable intelligence, which it is.

They are careful with the daughter, whose name is not for this page. Lucia meets her when the girl decides, not before. Mateo does not use Lucia as a surprise. The heat wave broke. The platform went back to being a platform. What remained was seven o'clock, a kiosk, and two adults who shared a bottle without inventing a history to justify it. They are making a history now, in ordinary weather, which is harder and better. The train is still late sometimes. They are less so.

**End.**
