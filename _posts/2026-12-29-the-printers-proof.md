The red was wrong. It was the red of a warning sign, and Poppy needed the red of a tomato in a market, which is a sentence a printer hears and then deserves a medal for not mocking. Colin did not mock it. He was forty-six and had mixed more reds than he had eaten meals. She was twenty-eight and on a deadline for a grocer who believed in exact fruit.

They stayed after the shop closed. The press ticked as it cooled. Colin pulled another sheet. Poppy held it to the window and shook her head, not unkindly, just accurately.

"Again," she said. "Less brown. I know you know. I am saying it so I feel useful."

"You are useful," Colin said. "You are also right. I was hoping the brown was a trick of my eyes. Forty-six-year-old eyes enjoy a trick. We will do it properly."

The proper red arrived on the fifth pull. The grocer would be satisfied. The shop was quiet in the way of a place that has done its job. Poppy sat on a stool and ate a sandwich she had forgotten she packed. Colin made tea that had nothing to do with color management.

"I do not stay late with clients," he said. "I stayed because the problem was real and because you did not treat my trade as a vending machine. If you want a meal that is not a sandwich over a bin, I am free tomorrow. If you want the invoice and nothing else, the red is still yours."

"Tomorrow," Poppy said. "Twenty-eight. I want the meal. I also want to keep paying you properly. Do not fold the invoice into the invitation."

"The invoice will arrive like a professional. The invitation already did."

The meal was in a place with no design to speak of, which relaxed them both. Poppy talked about a degree and a shared flat and a wish to do fewer logos and more books. Colin talked about an apprentice he was proud of and a marriage that had ended without a villain. He mentioned the age difference the way he mentioned a paper size, as a spec. She nodded and did not flinch, and she did not pretend it was nothing. They set a pace that respected both facts.

The grocer's red is on a window in town. Their own red is a kettle in the print shop after hours, and a rule they kept: the work gets an invoice, the evening gets a yes or a no, and neither is used to pay for the other. Colin still pulls proofs until the fruit looks like fruit. Poppy still says again, without apology. It is a good way to spend a life. The color matches. So, deliberately, do they.

**End.**
