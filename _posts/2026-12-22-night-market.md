The tourist wanted the dish explained in two languages and then wanted it without cilantro, which was not a translation problem so much as a wish. Wei handled it. He was thirty. He had stopped at Lin's stall because the line was short and the smell was serious. She was thirty-two and had heard every version of a stranger being helpful. Most of them got in the way. Wei stood to the side, said one sentence, and then ordered his own food like a person.

"You can go," Lin said, not unkindly. "The cilantro crisis is over."

"I am going to the end of the alley to eat," Wei said. "I am Wei. If you need a second pair of hands when you close, I will still be in the alley. If you do not, the bowl is enough. You do not owe me a job."

She watched him eat. He did not watch her work. That was the detail. At closing, a tarp fought her and a bulb had blown. She looked down the alley. He was there, rinsing his bowl at the public tap like a man with nowhere to perform.

"The tarp," Lin said. "Not the whole life. Just the tarp."

They folded it. Wei changed the bulb because he could reach and because she pointed. The street thinned out to hoses and laughter and the last scooters. Lin sat on a crate and ate the bowl she had saved. She offered him the extra egg. He took half.

"I translate manuals," Wei said. "Nights are for walking. I am not a character in a travel piece. Thirty. I live two stops away."

"Lin. Thirty-two. I do this six nights. On the seventh I sleep like a person in a story. If you want to walk the two stops on a night I close early, Monday, you may. Bring nothing but your hands, which were useful and not possessive. That is a review."

"I accept the review and the Monday."

Mondays became a route. Wei learned the names of the vendors and did not translate unless asked. Lin learned he sent money to a sister and kept little, and that this was a plan, not a plight. She had a plan of her own, a second stall, and he asked the questions that made the plan sturdier rather than the ones that made him essential. When the second stall opened, he came as a customer and waited in the actual line. She liked him most then.

They close the first street together still, not every night, on the nights he can be a pair of hands and she wants them. The market is loud and then it is hoses and quiet. Lin and Wei walk the two stops. Some weeks he stays. Some weeks he does not. The egg is still split when there is an extra. A small, fair portion. It suits them.

**End.**
