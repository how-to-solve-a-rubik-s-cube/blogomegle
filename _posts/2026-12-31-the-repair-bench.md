The camera had belonged to Yoko's work and then, after the magazine closed, to her alone. A shutter stuck. A shop in Shibuya told her to buy a new body. She took the train to a smaller bench she had heard about from a friend who still believed in screws. Haruto looked at the camera for a long minute. He was fifty-three. She was forty-eight and prepared to be dismissed.

"I can free the shutter," he said. "I cannot make it young. If you want young, the other shop was right. If you want this one, leave it for three days. I am Haruto."

"I want this one. I am Yoko. It has a scratch I like. Do not polish the scratch."

"I do not polish other people's history."

She came back on the third day. The shutter clicked with a sound she had missed in her hands. Haruto had written a note about a light seal she should watch and had not upsold her a strap. The bill was fair. She paid it and then stood in the narrow shop while a train went over the street and the light changed.

"I am going to photograph the river this week," Yoko said. "Not for a client. If the seal fails I will be back anyway. If it does not, I would still like to show you one frame. You do not have to want that. The repair is complete."

"The repair is complete," Haruto said. "Bring the frame. I close at seven. I like a picture of a river. I like knowing what the machine did after it left my bench."

The frame was good: water, a bicycle, a color that was only there for a minute. Haruto looked at it longer than politeness required and said, "You waited for the light." Yoko, who had been waiting for someone to notice that she waited, felt the sentence land. They went for noodles when the shop closed, a thing he suggested as a man who ate dinner, not as a reward system. He talked about an apprentice and a wife who had died eight years ago and a habit of going to the river himself on Mondays. She talked about the magazine and the year after it and the decision not to become a person who only mourned a job.

They go to the river on some Mondays now, two cameras sometimes, sometimes none. The shutter still sticks in humid months. Haruto frees it and does not sigh. Yoko has stopped explaining why she will not buy the new body. The reason is on the bench and in the kitchen they share: a life does not have to be replaced to be continued. It has to be cared for by someone who will not polish out the scratch.

**End.**
