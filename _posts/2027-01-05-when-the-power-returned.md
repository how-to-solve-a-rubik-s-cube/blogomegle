The power went at nine, and the building made a collective groan and then a collective migration onto the steps, where the air was only slightly less like a held breath. Carmen had a pitcher of water and paper cups. She was twenty-six and had bought the pitcher for a party that had never happened, so the blackout was its debut. Hector took a cup and sat one step down, not crowding. He was forty-three. They had nodded in the mailroom for a year.

"If this lasts, the ice will be a tragedy," Carmen said. "I am Carmen. I am going to talk because silence plus heat makes me invent problems. You can be a stranger with a cup. That is allowed."

"Hector. I will be a stranger with a cup who also talks. I work early. I was going to sleep. The dark is a change of plan, not a disaster. Forty-three. The heat is the disaster."

They talked about nothing important: a brother, a shift, a dog that belonged to the building and to no one. When the lights returned, a cheer went up and people filed inside toward refrigerators and televisions. Carmen stayed. Hector stayed. The step was still warm. The street had not finished being interesting.

"I do not want to go up yet," she said. "If you have a morning, you should go. If you do not, I have more water and no agenda."

"I have a morning," Hector said. "I am staying twenty minutes anyway. A morning can survive twenty minutes of a person I would like to see when the lights are on."

They used the twenty. Then they set a time for Saturday, in daylight, at a diner that had survived the outage and was smug about it. Hector came from his shift with clean hands and a tired face he did not disguise. Carmen came from a class she was taking in bookkeeping, a fact she was proud of and said so. The age sat on the table. She named it. He agreed it was real and not a verdict. They would go at the speed of Saturdays, not at the speed of a blackout, which makes people intimate and then embarrassed.

The Saturdays accumulated. The building's power has failed once more since, for ten minutes, and they laughed and did not reenact the steps. They were already inside, at her table, with the pitcher between them for ordinary thirst. Staying a little longer, that first night, was the whole bravery. Everything after has been keeping a promise made when the lights came back and they did not use the light as an excuse to scatter.
