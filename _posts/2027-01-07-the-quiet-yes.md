The dog was a question with paws. A neighbor was moving and could not take her, and Amy had sat on the kitchen floor with the dog's chin on her knee and looked up at Cole with a face that was already an answer and was still, politely, a question. She was thirty-four. He was thirty-six. The lease on the larger flat was on the counter, unsigned. The Tuesday ahead was ordinary and about to be less so.

"I am saying yes to the dog," Amy said. "I am saying yes to the lease if you are. I am saying yes to a Tuesday that has a walk in it even when we are tired. I am not saying yes to a version of us that needs an audience. If your yes is the same size, sign. If it is smaller, say the size."

Cole sat on the floor so the dog would not have to referee from above. "My yes is the same size," he said. "The dog, the lease, the tired walk. I will forget a walk someday and you will remind me and I will go. That is the clause. I want the clause. I want you. I have wanted you in this kitchen for a year and I am glad the dog made us say it at floor level."

They signed at the table, with the dog sighing as if the paperwork were beneath her. The larger flat was two blocks away and had a yard that was mostly mud and potential. They moved on a weekend with friends and too many blankets. Tuesday arrived, as Tuesdays do, and they took the walk in a wind that made the dog ridiculous and happy. Amy laughed. Cole put the laugh in the category of things he intended to keep hearing.

There was no party. There was a call to each set of parents and a photograph that was slightly out of focus and completely sufficient. The quiet yes turned out to be louder than the proposals they had seen, because it included chores. Cole cleans the mud. Amy does the morning walk when his shift starts early. They trade without a board meeting. Once a month they sit on the same kitchen floor, no crisis, just to be at the height where the decision was made. The dog considers this her achievement. They let her. She is not wrong. She was the occasion. The yes was theirs, and it was specific, and it has held through mud, wind, and the long stretch of ordinary Tuesdays that are, in the end, what a life actually is.

**End.**
