The lobby had a bench, a radiator, and a bowl of hard candy no one admitted to taking. Flora took one every afternoon on her way to the garden and nodded at the man who collected his mail at the same time. He nodded back. This went on until the nods were a relationship and also, she decided, not enough. She was seventy-six. He was seventy-three, though she did not know the number yet.

"I am Flora, in 3B," she said, stopping the nod mid-air. "I have tea at four. I am asking you to it tomorrow, not forever. If you would rather keep the mail as our whole story, say so and I will like you at this distance."

"Duncan, in 1A. I would like the tea. I will bring a biscuit that is not from the bowl. The bowl is a museum."

He arrived at four with shortbread in a packet and his shoes wiped, details Flora collected as evidence of character. Her flat was full of books stacked sideways and a view of a stone wall that she defended as calming. Duncan praised the wall without irony. They drank the tea. He had taught geography and still had a globe that was out of date, which he knew. She had run a dress shop and still noticed a hem. She noticed his. It was fine. She told him so, and he looked startled into pleasure.

"I have been nodding at you since October," he said. "I thought you might think a man in a lobby was a risk. I am a risk only to a packet of biscuits."

"I am a good judge of risk," Flora said. "You may come on Thursdays. Other days you must ask, so the tea does not become a duty with a saucer."

He asked, and not only on Thursdays. They went to a film and disliked it usefully, which is a fine early outing because it gives you a common enemy. Duncan met Flora's granddaughter and survived the inspection. Flora met Duncan's sister and compared notes on his inability to throw away a map. The sister was relieved. Someone else had noticed.

Four o'clock is still the hinge of the day. Sometimes the tea is in 3B and sometimes in 1A, where the globe has been updated with a sticker Flora finds undignified and correct. They keep their own leases. The building is not ready to be a single household, and neither are they, every hour. What they were ready for was an end to the nods. The radiator still knocks. The candy bowl is still a museum. Duncan and Flora are no longer part of the display. They are upstairs, on time, pouring a second cup because the first one went cold while they were laughing.

**End.**
