The list was on a clipboard by the kettle, and the kettle was the real center of the room. Aoife wrote her name next to Friday nights. She was thirty-three and had started volunteering because her weeks had become a corridor of work and streaming and she wanted a third thing that had a door. Niall's name was already on Friday. He was thirty-six, a sound engineer, and he had the kettle going before the shift lead arrived.

They were assigned the same table: tea, sugar, a listening face. The work was not dramatic. It was repetitive and specific, which Aoife was good at, and which Niall respected by not adding flourishes. At the end, chairs up, he washed while she dried, a division that happened without a meeting.

"You do not rush people," Niall said. "I noticed because I do, and I am trying not to. I am Niall. If you want the feedback stopped, say so. If you want a bag of chips on the walk to the bus, I am walking that way."

"Aoife. The feedback can stay. The chips can happen. I am not a project. I am a person who liked the way you handed over the sugar without making a joke about it."

The chips were salty and the bus was late. They talked about a sister of hers who thought volunteering was a personality, and a father of his who was proud in a way that did not ask questions. Aoife worked in a planning office. Niall worked nights when the gigs were there and days when they were not. Friday was the stable point. They put their names down for a month, then for another, not as a couple, as two people who made the shift better.

The couple part came in the chip shop, weeks later, when Niall said he had stopped signing up for Saturdays because he wanted one evening that was not a rota and he wanted to know if she wanted it too. Aoife said she did, and that they would not talk about the shelter the entire time, because the people there were not material. He agreed so quickly she knew he had hoped for the rule.

They still do Fridays. The clipboard still has their names, a line apart or together depending on who grabs the pen. Saturdays are a film or a walk or a sofa, and the tiredness is a different animal, the good kind, from being awake in a life. Kindness got them into the same room. Choosing got them the rest of the week. Aoife is clear about the order. Niall repeats it when anyone tries to make the shelter into a matchmaking service. It is not. It is a door. They walked through it, and then they walked to a bus, and then they decided the bus was not the only place they were going.

**End.**
