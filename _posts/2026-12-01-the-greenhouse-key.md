The key was on a green tag, and Iris did not lend it lightly. She was forty-seven, a botanist who closed the tropical house at dusk and knew every person who had ever asked to stay a little longer. Malcolm was fifty-one. He had asked without poetry. His mother was dying in a nearby ward, and the hospital chapel smelled like carpet, and he had remembered that glasshouses were warm.

"I cannot give you the run of the place," Iris said. "I can walk you through at six, off the clock, for twenty minutes. If you need that more than once, you will have to meet me, not a key left under a pot. I am Iris."

"Malcolm. Twenty minutes is a fortune. I will meet you. I do not want a key that outlives the reason."

They walked the twenty minutes. The air was wet and green and slightly rotten in the way of living things, which Malcolm found more comforting than lilies. He did not talk about the ward inside the glass. He talked about it on the path after, where the ordinary cold gave him a boundary. Iris listened and did not translate his mother into a lesson. She said she was sorry. She said six tomorrow was possible. She said she was a person who went home to a flat in Richmond and a radio play, so he would not mistake her for a spirit of the garden.

His mother died on a Thursday. Malcolm came on Friday anyway, because the twenty minutes had become a place to put his body. Iris had expected him. She had two teas in paper cups. They did not go into the tropical house. They sat on a bench by a hedge and let the tea go cold at the edges.

"I do not know what I am asking for now," he said.

"Then do not ask yet," Iris said. "Come on Fridays. We will look at something that is not required to get better. I like the alpine house. It is full of plants that chose a hard climate and got on with it."

They looked at alpines. Malcolm learned their names slowly, which Iris preferred to a man who memorized in order to impress her. She learned he taught physics at a school and had taken leave, and that he was ready to go back, and that going back frightened him only because it meant the emergency was over and the ordinary would have to hold. She said the ordinary was her whole field. A plant that returns in spring is not dramatic. It is the work.

By June the leave was over and the Fridays remained. Iris gave him a key then, not to the greenhouses, which were not hers to give, but to the gate of a community plot she kept. He used it to water when she was at a conference. He did not move a single label. She noticed. The notice became dinner, then a weekend, then a decision to stop calling the plot a neutral place. The key on the green tag stayed at the garden, on its hook, for the job. The other key is on Malcolm's ring, beside the school fob. He still asks before he comes by on a bad day. She still says yes, and sometimes she says not tonight, and both answers are part of the trust. The plants do not mind either way. They come up regardless.
