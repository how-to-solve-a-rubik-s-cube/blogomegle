Everyone else had done the decent thing and gone home. The truck was returned. The pizza was a theory. One box remained in the middle of the new room, labeled in Lauren's handwriting: MISC, which meant she had given up at hour six. She was thirty-four. Brett was still on the floor, back against the wall, thirty-eight, a friend from work who had lifted more than a friend had to and had not once checked his phone as a hint.

"You can go," Lauren said. "The box is books. Books are not an emergency."

"I am staying through the books," Brett said. "If I leave, you will use them as a table and never unpack a life. I have seen you do this with mail. I am fond of you and opposed to the method."

The word fond sat in the empty room. Lauren opened the box. They shelved in silence for a few minutes, which was not avoidance, just dust and decisions about alphabetizing. Then she said, "Fond is a careful word. I am careful too. I asked you to help because I trust you with the heavy things. I did not ask the rest. I am asking now. Stay for a drink after the books. If fond is the ceiling, tell me before I pour."

"Fond is the floor," Brett said. "I did not want to make a move in a truck. Trucks make people agree to things they only mean for the afternoon. I mean a week, and then another, if the drink goes well. Thirty-eight. No performance."

The drink was warm soda from a cooler, because the glasses were in a different box and the honesty did not require ice. They sat against the wall. Lauren talked about the job she was leaving and the one she was starting, and the fear that a new city block would only be the old loneliness with different light. Brett talked about a divorce that was three years finished and a habit of being useful so he would not have to be a guest. She asked him to be a guest in the new place, not a mover. He put his hands on his knees and said he could try, and that she should tell him if the usefulness crept back in.

It crept, once, and she told him, and he sat down on purpose. That was the whole repair. The box labeled MISC got emptied. The books look like a person's books. Brett has a key he uses as a guest who is also the man she loves, a combination they defined out loud so the lock would not have to. Austin at night is loud on that street. Inside, the last box is broken down and recycled. Nothing is miscellaneous anymore. The important items have names.

**End.**
