The board was a piece of cork and a chaos of paper. Felix pinned his note at eye level and immediately disliked the pin, the cork, and himself. He was thirty. The note said: You had a green coat and a broken suitcase wheel. You told me the time without looking at your phone. I was the man with the orange ticket and the bad question about the ring line. If you want a coffee that is not on a platform, write to the address below. If you do not, the wheel may the suitcase forgive you anyway. He had left the grammar. He did not go back to fix it.

Anja saw the note two days later because the wheel was still broken and she was back at the same station, irritated with a repair shop. She was twenty-eight. She read the note twice. She almost left it for someone greener. Then she recognized the bad question. He had asked whether the ring line really made a ring, and she had said yes, and also no, and he had looked delighted by the honesty.

She wrote, not to the board, to the address. I am the coat. The wheel is still a disgrace. Coffee is acceptable. I do not want a scene. Anja.

They met at a place near the canal where the cups were thick and the tables tipped. Felix stood when she arrived and then sat, because standing felt like a play. Anja put the suitcase where she could see it.

"I almost did not write," she said. "Notes on boards are usually a wish to be a story. I came because your question was sincere. Do not become insincere now that I am here. Thirty is what the note implied. I am twenty-eight."

"Thirty exactly. I will stay sincere even when it makes me dull. I work in a bike shop. The orange ticket was a day pass I did not need. I bought it to have a reason to stand there long enough to talk to you. That is the least heroic version."

"Good," Anja said. "I design exhibitions. I like the least heroic version of a person. It is the one that shows up again."

He showed up again on Saturday, with a wheel he had replaced himself, at a shop, on his own time, and he gave it to her as a part, not as a metaphor. She installed it while he handed tools and did not take over. The suitcase rolled. They tested it along the canal until the testing was obviously a walk. Anja told him about a brother in Leipzig. Felix told her he had moved to Berlin for a person and stayed for the city after the person left. She did not require the details. He offered them once, briefly, and then asked about her exhibition schedule.

The board note came down with the monthly clearing. Neither of them saved it. They saved Saturday. The wheel is an ordinary wheel. The ring line is still a ring and not a ring, depending on where you get off. They get off together more often than not, and when they do not, the absence is a plan, not a miss. The cork can hold other people's wishes. Theirs has a table.

**End.**
